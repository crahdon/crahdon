Coursework Project

Electro-Pneumatic Pipe Organ

GENED 1080: How Technology Works — Harvard University  |  Prof. Robert Wood

Overview

This project involved designing and building a functional 10-pipe electro-pneumatic organ from scratch, integrating acoustic physics, pneumatic systems, and embedded electronics into a single working instrument.

My Contributions

  • Designed and built the solenoid control circuits, pivoting from breadboard to a fully soldered solution after identifying connection instability under load
  • Diagnosed and resolved a power limitation where a 9V battery could only actuate 1–2 valves simultaneously under pressure by switching to a 6V regulated supply capable of firing all 10 at once
  • Built the pneumatic distribution system connecting the wall compressor to the manifold and pipes
  • Constructed the wooden mounting frame for the pipe assembly

How It Works

The 10 pipes were modelled in SolidWorks and 3D-printed in PLA, with dimensions calculated from first principles using f = v/2L. Tuning caps were added to drop each pipe's pitch by an octave, improving tonal clarity and reducing the required air pressure. Compressed air is distributed via a PVC manifold and controlled by 10 solenoid valves, each triggered independently by an Arduino microcontroller.
